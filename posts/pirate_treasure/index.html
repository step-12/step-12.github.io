    Pirate Treasure

    August 19, 2026
    Puzzle, Fun, Treasure

    The Pirate’s Treasure Map#

    A Message from an Old Pirate#

    A long time ago, a pirate buried a treasure somewhere on a distant island.

    Before disappearing, he left behind a strange map.

    It does not show mountains, rivers, or paths. Instead, it contains rows of numbers. The pirate claimed that anyone clever enough to follow the map would eventually discover the coordinates of his treasure.

    Unfortunately, the map is written in a peculiar code.

    Your job is to decipher it.

    The Map#

    Here is the treasure map:

    Cell | Contents
-----+---------
   1 | 3 9 0 0
   5 | 0 1 4 5
   9 | 1 4 8 4
  13 | 1 5 6 5
  17 | 5 5 7 9
  21 | 9 0 0 0

    The number on the left tells you the first cell number in that row.

    For example:

    5 | 0 1 4 5

    means:

    Cell 5 = 0
Cell 6 = 1
Cell 7 = 4
Cell 8 = 5

    Similarly:

    13 | 1 5 6 5

    means:

    Cell 13 = 1
Cell 14 = 5
Cell 15 = 6
Cell 16 = 5

    Every cell contains a number.

    Some numbers are ordinary values. Others are instructions telling you what to do next.

    Your journey always begins at cell 1.

    How to Read the Map#

    The pirate has used four different instructions.

    Each instruction tells you what to do and, importantly, which cell contains the next instruction.

    The instructions can change this path by telling you to jump to a different cell.

    Instruction 1 — Add#

    An instruction beginning with 1 tells you to add the values stored in two cells and put the result into another cell.

    It has this form:

    1 [First Cell] [Second Cell] [Result Cell]

    For example:

    1 10 20 30

    means:

    Take the value in cell 10 and the value in cell 20. Add them together and put the result in cell 30.

    The values in cells 10 and 20 are not changed.

    The value in cell 30 is replaced by the result.

    Where do you go next?#

    To go to the next instruction, add four to the current cell you are executing

    For example:

    11 | 1 2 3 4
15 | x x x x

    If you are currently executing cell number 11 which is an add instruction, then the next cell to execute is current cell(11) + 4 = 15. So the next instruction to execute will be at cell number 15.

    Instruction 3 — Jump#

    An instruction beginning with 3 tells you to continue reading the map from another cell.

    It has this form:

    3 [Cell Number]

    For example:

    3 15

    means:

    Continue reading the map from cell 15.

    Where do you go next?#

    After carrying out a Jump instruction, do not go to the next cell.

    Go directly to the cell specified by the instruction.

    For example, if the instruction

    3 15

    is in cell 7, the next instruction is in cell 15.

    Instruction 5 — Jump If Less Than#

    An instruction beginning with 5 compares the values in two cells.

    It has this form:

    5 [First Cell] [Second Cell] [Target Cell]

    For example:

    5 10 20 15

    means:

    Look at the values in cells 10 and 20.

    If the value in cell 10 is smaller than the value in cell 20, continue from cell 15.

    If it is not smaller, continue normally.

    Where do you go next?#

    There are two possibilities.

    If the first value is smaller than the second value:

    Go directly to the target cell.

    For example, if the instruction is in cell 7:

    5 10 20 15

    and cell 10 contains a smaller value than cell 20, the next instruction is in cell 15.

    If the first value is not smaller than the second value:

    Add four to the current cell you are executing.

    For example:

    11 | 5 10 20 15
    1. You are currently on cell 11
    2. You determine that the contents of cell 10 are not lesser than the contents of cell 20
    3. Your next instruction is current cell(11) + 4 = 15

    Instruction 9 — Stop#

    Instruction 9 means:

    Stop.

    Where do you go next?#

    Nowhere.

    The map has been completely followed, and the journey ends here.

    Example#

    Example:

    1  | 1 2  3 1
5  | 1 2  4 6
9  | 1 4  5 6
13 | 3 16 0 9
17 | 0 0  0 0
    1. Execution starts at cell 1.
    • Add contents of cell 2 to cell 3 and put the result in cell 1
    • cell 2 contains 2, cell 3 contains 3, so 2 + 3 = 5
    • cell 1 should contain 5. Replace cell 1 with 5
    • current cell we are executing is 1. Next cell is 1 + 4 = 5
    1. Execution continues at cell number 5
    • Add contents of cell 2 to cell 4 and put the result in cell 6
    • cell 2 contains 2, cell 4 contains 1, so 2 + 1 = 3
    • cell 6 should contain 3. Replace cell 6 with 3
    • current cell we are executing is 5. Next cell is 5 + 4 = 9.
    1. Execution continues at cell number 9
    • Add contents of cell 4 to cell 5 and put the result in cell 6
    • cell 4 contains 1, cell 5 contains 1, so 1 + 1 = 2
    • cell 6 should contain 2. Replace cell 6 with 2
    • current cell we are executing is 9. Next cell is 9 + 4 = 13.
    1. Execution continutes at cell number 13
    • Instruction number is 3, which means a jump
    • Current cell we are executing is 13. It asks us to jump to cell 16
    • Next cell to execute is 16
    1. Execution continues at cell number 16
    • Instruction number is 9 which means halt
    • Stop executing instructions

    Final result

    1  | 5 2  3 1
5  | 1 2  4 6
9  | 1 4  5 6
13 | 3 16 0 9
17 | 0 0  0 0

    One Important Detail#

    The map is not fixed while you are reading it.

    An Add instruction can change the value of a cell.

    This means that a cell you looked at earlier may contain a different value when you encounter it again.

    For example, suppose cell 20 initially contains 7.

    Later, an Add instruction might place 25 into cell 20.

    From that point onward, the value of cell 20 is 25.

    So don’t just follow the path.

    Keep track of the values in the cells as they change.

    Your Mission#

    The pirate has hidden the treasure’s coordinates inside the map.

    Your task is to discover them.

    Start at cell 1 and follow the instructions exactly as described.

    As you proceed:

    1. Read the instruction in your current cell.
    2. Carry out the instruction.
    3. Record any values that change.
    4. Determine which cell contains the next instruction.
    5. Continue until you reach instruction 9.

    When the map finally stops, look at:

    Cell 4 and Cell 5.

    The values in these two cells are the coordinates of the pirate’s treasure.

    The Question#

    What are the treasure’s coordinates?

    Don’t guess.

    Follow the map carefully, keep track of every change, and let the pirate’s instructions lead you to the treasure.

    Tip: Use Pencil and Paper#

    This puzzle is much easier to solve with pencil and paper.

    Write the entire grid out on a sheet of paper. Then use the tip of your pencil to point at the cell containing the instruction you are currently following.

    Each time you finish an instruction, move the pencil to the cell containing the next instruction.

    When an Add instruction changes the value in a cell, use your eraser to remove the old value and write down the new one.

    This gives you a simple way to keep track of both:

    • Where you are on the map.
    • How the values in the cells change as you follow the instructions.

    Don’t try to keep the entire map in your head. Let the pencil do the remembering for you.

    Problems#

    Write a program to find the greater of two numbers. Your program should begin with these numbers:

    1  | 3 13 15 11
5  | 0 0  0 0
9  | 0 0  0 0
13 |

    You will begin writing your program from cell number 13. The two numbers you need to compare are in cell 3 and cell 4. The result is expected in cell 5.

    Assignment#

    Write a program to find the greatest of three numbers. Your program should begin with these numbers:

    1  | 3 13 2 4
5  | 1 0  0 0
9  | 0 0  0 0
13 |

    You will begin writing your program from cell number 13. Your inputs are in cells 3,4 and 5. The result is expected in cell 6.

    Your program is expected to work regardless of the inputs. Assume any positive numbers might be provided as input. Assume that no two numbers are equal.
