4. The FPS-squared challenge

Compare two ways of describing the same acceleration. Set a physical gravity value, then see what frame-based gravity it corresponds to at different frame rates.

Why does FPS get squared?

Velocity has units of pixels/second. Acceleration tells us how much velocity changes per second, so its units are pixels/second².

When converting frame-based acceleration, there are two “per frame” factors to convert: one from the change in velocity, and one from the time interval over which that change happens.

a (px/s²) = a (px/frame²) × FPS²

So the reverse conversion is:

a (px/frame²) = a (px/s²) ÷ FPS²

Velocity only has one time factor:

v (px/s) = v (px/frame) × FPS

Position itself is measured in pixels, so it is not multiplied by FPS. In a time-based update, position changes by velocity × elapsed time, and velocity changes by acceleration × elapsed time.
